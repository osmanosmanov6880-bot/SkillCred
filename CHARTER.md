# SkillCred Community Charter

## 1. Mission

SkillCred exists to make evidence of practical skills portable, verifiable and usable across independent organizations. It must not become a compulsory identity system, a surveillance system, or a single authority that decides a person's worth.

## 2. Foundational commitments

1. **Human autonomy:** credentials belong to their holders; participation is voluntary except where a separate, lawful process says otherwise.
2. **Privacy by default:** collect and publish the minimum data needed. Recipient identifiers are optional in the MVP.
3. **Open interoperability:** publish technical specifications and permit independent implementations under the project license and applicable law.
4. **Due process:** participants can challenge errors and decisions through an accessible process.
5. **No retaliation for dissent:** criticism, lawful disagreement or proposing an alternative is not a valid ground for punishment.
6. **Security and honesty:** do not claim certification, conformance or security guarantees that have not been independently verified.
7. **Distributed stewardship:** no founder, donor, customer or related group has permanent or unchecked control.
8. **Right to exit:** participants can export their data and continue using credentials with compatible alternatives.
9. **Proportionality:** any restrictions must have a clear purpose, evidence of need, limited scope and review path.
10. **Continuous correction:** errors and unintended harms are recorded, investigated and corrected openly where safe.

## 3. Prohibited project uses

The project must not intentionally be used by its maintainers to enable stalking, covert surveillance, coercive social scoring, discriminatory exclusion, deceptive credential claims, retaliation against critics, or unreviewable centralized control. A credential is evidence of a specific achievement; it is not an overall score of human value.

## 4. Governance

A participant council, technical council and independent review mechanism exercise distinct responsibilities. The founder has no permanent veto and no right to make unilateral charter amendments. Membership criteria, appointment process, terms and conflict-of-interest disclosures must be public.

## 5. Amendments

- Publish proposed amendments with rationale and impact assessment.
- Allow at least 30 days for public comment, except emergency changes strictly necessary to address an active security issue; emergency changes receive retrospective public review.
- Publish a disposition of material objections.
- Fundamental amendments require a two-thirds vote of the participant council and any additional approval required by the project's legal structure.
- Amendments may not remove privacy, due-process, non-retaliation or right-to-exit commitments without a new, separately reviewed public process and independent legal/ethical assessment.

## 6. Enforcement and remedy

A violation report goes to an unconflicted reviewer. The reporter and affected parties receive a reasonable opportunity to be heard. Outcomes must be proportionate and reasoned, with appeal to an independent reviewer. Public reporting must protect personal data and avoid exposing exploitable security details.

## 7. Review

Review this charter annually with independent participants. Publish changes, unresolved objections and evidence of how the project met or failed its commitments.
